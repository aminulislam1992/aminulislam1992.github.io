About Me

I am a graduate research assistant at Tuskegee University, pursuing an M.S. in Materials Science and Engineering under the supervision of Dr. Walter Malone. My research is funded by the NSF DMREF Award #2324157, a collaborative project focused on the computationally driven discovery and synthesis of 2D materials through selective etching.

My work sits at the intersection of machine learning and computational materials science. I use universal machine-learned interatomic potentials (UMA, MACE) coupled with high-throughput screening workflows to accelerate the discovery of new MXenes and 2D materials — achieving predictions that are 10⁴–10⁶× faster than traditional DFT while maintaining chemical accuracy.

Prior to graduate school, I spent over six years in industry at Walton Hi-Tech Industries PLC in Bangladesh, where I worked as a Product Design Manager and filed a patent for an antimicrobial freezer liner.

Featured Research

High-Throughput Screening of MAX Phase Exfoliation Energies

Built an ASE workflow using UMA and MACE universal potentials to screen 243 M₂AX, M₃AX₂, and M₄AX₃ carbide and nitride compositions — predicting exfoliation energies, surface terminations, vacancy formation energies, and formation enthalpies at 10⁴–10⁶× the speed of DFT.

Paper (In Prep) Code

Oxidation-Driven Selectivity in MAX Phase Etching

Collaborative study with Dr. Klyukin's group applying high-throughput uMLIP screening of 162 carbide compositions to understand oxidation-driven selectivity in MAX phase etching for MXene synthesis.

Paper (In Prep)

Etching-Aware ML Framework for MXene Bandgap Prediction

Developing a physics-informed machine learning framework that incorporates DOS fingerprinting, delta-learning, and termination sensitivity mapping for accurate MXene bandgap prediction.

Paper (In Prep)

Stable MXene Surface Termination Search

Using Monte Carlo and genetic algorithms with uMLIP energies to explore stable MXene surface terminations, with lowest-energy candidates validated by Quantum ESPRESSO DFT for energy storage, electronics, and catalysis applications.
